Keething is a KeePass password manager for Android. It opens the same .kdbx file you use with KeePassXC, KeePass or Strongbox on your computer.

The database is a file in a folder you pick. Syncthing, Nextcloud or another sync app keeps it in step across your devices, and Keething picks up what they bring. There is no account, the app has no network permission, and it collects nothing. It is free and open source.

<b>Sync</b>
• Each save reads the file again and changes only the entry you edited, so what you changed on your other devices meanwhile stays.
• When Syncthing ends up with two versions of the file, the app shows both and you pick one.
• Keething keeps backups of each database on the phone, and you can restore one from the database's page.

<b>Entries</b>
• Passwords in a monospaced font where similar characters are easy to tell apart, with digits and symbols in colour. One-time codes count down next to them.
• A payment card looks like a card. Bank accounts show their IBAN, passports and IDs their expiry date. Wi-Fi networks and SSH logins get their own layout too.
• For a crypto wallet, every word of the recovery phrase is checked against BIP-39. The phrase can't be copied, shared or shown as a QR code. The receiving address shows its network and a QR code.
• Notes, custom fields, attachments, tags, and each entry's history, where you can go back to an earlier version.

<b>Every day</b>
• Unlock with your fingerprint.
• Search as you type, by group (@work) and by tag (#finance) too.
• All your two-factor codes on one screen. Add a code by pasting it or scanning its QR code.
• Autofill in apps and browsers. After you sign in, it offers to save the password.
• A password generator.
• Several databases open at once, key files, and new databases created on the phone.
• Copied passwords don't show in the clipboard preview, and the clipboard is cleared after 30 seconds.

<b>Sharing</b>
• Ask someone for an entry and get it back encrypted for your phone only. It can go through any messenger, by email, or as a QR code in person; there is no server in between. To show the request is really yours, you read out six pictures over a call.
• Show a password as a QR code or in large letters, for typing it on another device.

<b>Formats</b>
KDBX 4 with AES-256 or ChaCha20 and Argon2 or AES-KDF, as KeePassXC, KeePass and Strongbox write it. KDBX 3.1 (what KeePassDX creates by default) opens read-only. If you agree, it is upgraded to KDBX 4, and the old file stays next to it.

<b>Not supported yet</b>
Twofish, hardware keys such as YubiKey, Android versions before 14, and languages other than English.

<b>Source code</b>
GPL-3.0-or-later. The build is reproducible, so you can check that the APK comes from the published source. Code, issues and the security policy: https://github.com/cotton-more/keething-android
